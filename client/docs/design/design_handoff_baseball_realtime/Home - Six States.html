Home — six states

Each frame is the real screen, not a mock-up of one. Five of the six needed a design decision rather than just a screenshot; each is noted under its frame.

01Six things are hotAbove the section's ceiling

Needed a decision. Five show and the rest fold behind one “1 more” line. Letting six run makes this the news feed the page was designed against, and pushes Races below the fold for no gain. It is an expander, not a “view all” — there is no page of hot items to go to. Dropping the extras silently was the alternative and it's worse: you'd never know the day had more in it. Note the rail's height is a coincidence — eight follows and five hot items happen to come to the same ~430px; nothing links them, which is why Races now spans the full width below both rather than sitting in an 824px column beside empty rail.

02Nothing is hot, 15 games todayThe normal state before first pitch

Changed. The day ahead is now a time-ordered list capped at four, plus “All 15 games today →”. Fifteen cards would make Home a scoreboard — the one thing the page is defined against — and five card-shaped things read as a grid, not a shortlist. Which four is the generator's job (aces, stakes, rivalry), not “the first four by start time”, which on a 15-game Sunday is four one-o'clock games.

03Following nothingFirst run, every new user

The rail keeps its width and its head — collapsing it would reflow the whole page the moment you follow one thing, and an empty column is not the same as a missing one. One sentence and the button, which is the second entrance to the same Manage panel.

04It's JuneThe state the design didn't have

Needed a decision. There were only two season modes — April (quiet) and the run-in — which left most of the season homeless. June is a real third state: the standings mean something so the full board is right, but nothing is decided, nobody has clinched, and the cut line isn't yet a deadline. Two consequences: the section is called “Races” in every state (“September” only read right for one month of six; the season now lives in the note beside the label), and the membership rule changes. “Mathematically alive” filters nothing in June, so the rule that holds all season is within striking distance — a games-back threshold that tightens as games remaining falls, converging on “alive” by late September. One knob, 3–4 clubs per division here.

05One hot thing, eight followsWhere the dead space was

The dead-space case, fixed. One hot item is ~85px; a full rail is ~460px — and because Races spans the full width in row 2, the difference was a blank hole in the left column. The section now fills to about the rail's height with the day ahead (four rows minus the hot ones), under a quiet “Coming up today” label. It is not padding: those games are real, and on a quiet afternoon they are the answer to “what deserves my attention”. At three or more hot items the tail disappears — the column stands on its own.

06Following 20More than fits

Changed. The 8 cap was arbitrary — a dashboard of eight is a design, twenty is a list, and someone with twenty interests isn't misusing the feature. Cap is now 20, and the rail scrolls internally, bounded to the viewport: a sticky element taller than the viewport stops behaving like one (it pins at its own bottom, so the top of the list is unreachable while Races is on screen). The affordance is the shared EdgeButton, top and bottom — same atom as the widget slides and the Leaders tables, hidden at rest and revealed on hover, so the native scrollbar is suppressed rather than left as a second treatment. Three rows per press. Live rows sort to the top, which only starts mattering at this length: a live row below the fold is a row you won't see.
