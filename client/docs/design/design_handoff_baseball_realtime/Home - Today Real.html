The Bregman case, which is the useful one: it is not in What's hot and is in the news — because the generator could never have produced it. No news ingest; he was hit on deck, so there is no plate appearance, no pitch and no event enum; the only structured trace is a mid-game substitution, indistinguishable from a double switch. An earlier version of this page carried it as the lead hot item, which was a fiction — and its dedupe then suppressed the AP headline, hiding the one surface that would actually have carried the story.

In the news, at the foot of the page, comes from a news API rather than the game feeds — which is exactly why it is a separate section. What's hot is what our own feeds prove is happening (generated, carries a score or a race, IQ can answer questions about it); a headline is what someone else is claiming (fetched, carries a source and a link, no structured context). Merging them would put an IQ insight and an outside claim at equal weight, and you could no longer tell which is the app's own judgment. The dedupe you can see working is the Dodgers sweep: that one is generatable from the game feed, so the hot item wins and the ESPN headline drops.

Every hot item and the whole Races board are real as of today — leaders, games-back, berth-holders and clinches. Win–loss is omitted rather than faked: I do not have today's records, and a plausible-looking fake record is worse than a missing column.
