Line score band — four ways to give the space back

The band is fixed chrome: it never scrolls away and it never changes height, so it costs the same on every visit — including the one where you only want the feed. It measures — tall.

What it holds today, left to right: line score (9 innings + R/H/E, current inning in rust) · game leaders (2 slots, always reserved) · last pitch (moved here in July to shorten the matchup column). All four options below keep every one of those facts on the page. They differ in where the facts live and when you pay height for them.

Option 2 · Full at the top, condensed once you scroll

44px scrolledsaves all of it, below the first screen
What it buysThe band keeps its full form where you land, then collapses to a sticky 44px strip as you scroll into the feed. It also fixes the opposite problem: today, scrolling to reach the feed means the score leaves the screen entirely.
What it costsThe first screen is unchanged, so it only pays off once you scroll — and it adds a moving part (one transition, one sticky element) to a screen that currently has none.

Option 3 · Score bar always, line score on demand

measuring…saves —
What it buysSeparates the two jobs the band is doing. Score, inning, count, last pitch are live state — you want them every second, and they fit in one row. The inning grid and leaders are reference — you check them a few times a game, so they open on request and close again.
What it costsThe inning-by-inning read becomes a deliberate act instead of a glance. If you look at it more often than you think you do, this is the option that will annoy you.

Option 4 · No band at all

0pxsaves the whole band
What it buysEvery fact moves to somewhere it already belongs. Score + inning join the page header's status slot, next to LIVE — the slot the pattern already reserves for status. Last pitch moves into the feed's own pinned header, where the pitch it describes lives. The inning grid is the scorecard's top row — and the scorecard is already one tap away in the feed. Leaders go below the fold with the other analysis.
What it costsThe biggest change of the four, and it touches three components instead of one. The inning grid goes from always-visible to two steps away (open scorecard). Reverses the July decision that moved last pitch into the band.

If you want a recommendation

Option 3, and it's a clean fit for how you describe using the page: the row you never want to lose is score / inning / count / last pitch, and that row is 56px. The inning grid is the part you glance at occasionally, and it's the part costing you 60-odd pixels on every visit.

Option 2 is the safer sibling — nothing gets harder to reach, you just stop paying for it after the first screen — and the two combine if you want the resting bar and stickiness. Option 4 is where I'd go if you tell me you basically never read the inning grid, since the scorecard already draws it properly.
